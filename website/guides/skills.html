The Wheel of Skills

Eighteen skills, one pool of 300 points, and a character that is yours alone. Everyone can dabble in everything; almost nobody can master more than one thing.

The shape of it

Every skill runs 0 to 100. Level 0 means untouched. A character holds 300 points total, one point per level, so holding all eighteen skills at Novice costs 18 points, while one master costs a third of everything you have.

That last pair is the point of the design. Only three of your skills can rise above 75, and only one above 90, so nobody can be the best at everything. An ebony vein yields only to a Master Miner, so choose the trade you want to be known for.

The eighteen

Every skill on the Wheel, with what each of its five tiers does today. A tier starts at the level shown: Novice at 1, Apprentice at 25, Journeyman at 50, Expert at 75 and Master at 90. Eight skills open at a station; the other ten are taken up by doing the work. Your skills menu (K) shows the Wheel’s own description of each tier, and where it promises more than the tables below, the tables are what the game does now.

Destruction, Conjuration and Illusion, and Alteration for a mage who chooses it as a school. A mage follows one of four schools of magic, each with its own meter, and a tome must fit both your tier here and your rank in its school. Every spell you learn stays in your spellbook, and three are prepared at a time, whatever their school; you change them in the Synod Conclave or beside a Scholars’ Ledger. Taken up by doing it (the work banks until there is a level’s worth, then the skill is offered), or at once by choosing a school of magic; a Destruction, Conjuration or Illusion tome can be read only once you have a school. Fists, claws, quarterstaves and battle staves. Every punch that lands drains your opponent’s stamina (a power attack twice as much), and a tired fighter’s blows in close combat land much weaker, so a boxer who stays close can wear anyone down. Fists do little against armour, while a staff ignores a quarter of it. An unarmed power attack that lands knocks the weapon out of another player’s hands, at any tier. Taken up by doing it: the work banks until there is a level’s worth, then the skill is offered.

TierLevelLockpicking · The Quiet Hand
Novice1Novice chests
Apprentice25Apprentice chests
Journeyman50Adept chests
Expert75Expert chests
Master90Master chests

Opening what others locked. Only two kinds of lock can be picked: jail cell doors and locked chests in dungeons. Anyone with a lockpick may try a cell door, but only a Lockpicker can open a dungeon chest, and your tier decides the hardest chest you can attempt. Each tier holds the tumblers up longer and snaps fewer picks: a mistimed pick snaps 35% of the time at Novice and 11% at Master. The same quiet hand picks pockets: sneak up on another player and press X. The odds run from 30% at Novice to 70% at Master, better from behind and worse against a drawn weapon, and a lift takes either some of their coin or one item. Taken up by doing it: the work banks until there is a level’s worth, then the skill is offered.

Journeyman50Adept spells

Restoration and Alteration. The only way to learn healing and warding spells, studied at any spell study point. Alteration is shared with Arcane Arts: a priest learns it here, and a mage who chose Alteration as a school learns it through Arcane Arts. Praying at your god’s shrine counts as Priest work and raises the odds of a blessing, from 5% at Novice to 30% at Master; the higher your tier, the longer a blessing lasts, from 8 hours to 24. A Priest’s healing also lifts the chill of the grave from anyone who woke at the temple, and an Expert’s healing raises the fallen where they lie. Taken up by doing it (the work banks until there is a level’s worth, then the skill is offered), or at once by reading a Novice Restoration or Alteration tome at a spell study point.

Skills you learn by doing

A trade opens at its station: touch a forge and you are a smith, for one point. The other ten skills have no station to walk to, so they open through the work itself. Use one before you hold it and your effort is saved. Once it adds up to a level, the skill is offered to you:

“You have fought often enough this way to call it your own. Open your skills (K) to take up Martial Arts.”

Taking it up in your skills menu (K) costs one point, and the effort you saved is credited to it, up to two levels at once, so it pays to accept soon. Nothing is spent until you accept, so a stray arrow never costs you a point. If all 300 of your points are in use, the point comes from a skill you have marked Waning (see below).

How points are earned

Only real work counts, and the server checks every piece of it: a craft at a real bench, a vein you actually worked, a fight you actually fought. Bigger work counts for more, such as a costlier craft, a costlier spell or a richer ore.

Each skill can take in only so much work an hour, so steady play beats a frantic rush. Doing the same thing to the same target again and again counts for less: the ninth time within an hour is worth half, and logging out does not reset that. A starving character learns at a quarter pace. The Leveling Guide has the numbers.

How points are lost

Nothing fades while you are away. A skill only falls when another rises past your 300 points and has to take them from somewhere, and you choose where from under the two moons, Masser and Secunda. Mark each skill you hold in your skills menu (K):

◒Waxing
Rises with use. Gives way only when nothing is waning.
●Held
Never falls. Your trade is safe while you swing a sword.
◓Waning
The first to give way when something else rises.

Nothing ever drops below level 25. If no skill can give way, the rising skill simply stops and you are told to mark a skill to fall. Taking up a new skill with every point in use works the same way, except that only a Waning skill pays for it: the point comes from your highest Waning skill, and with nothing Waning you must mark one first.

Make your name on the Wheel

In most games you can become everything. Here you become someone. Every character has 300 points to spread across eighteen skills, from the mine and the forge to Blade, Blunt, Martial Arts, Arcane Arts and the Priest’s prayers. Dabble in all of them, or pour yourself into one. Everyone starts the alpha on the same footing, with every skill back at the beginning.

Learn by doing. Eight trades open the first time you set your hand to their station: an ore vein, a forge, an alchemy lab. The other ten, from Blade to Scholar, are learned by doing: fight with a sword and the effort banks as you go, and once there is a level’s worth you are offered the skill in your skills menu (K).

Mastery that means something. Only three of your skills can rise above 75, and only one can take the Seat above 90. Nobody can be the best at everything, so players who master different trades need each other.

Your progress is safe. Nothing fades while you are away. When a skill you hold rises past your 300, the Wheel takes the difference from another, and you choose which under Masser and Secunda: keep your trade Held, let an old habit Wane, and no skill is ever pulled below 25.

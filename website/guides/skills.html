The Wheel of Skills

Eighteen skills, one pool of 300 points, and a character that is yours alone. Everyone can dabble in everything; almost nobody can master more than one thing.

The shape of it

Every skill runs 0 to 100. Level 0 means untouched, and you take a trade up simply by using its station. A character holds 300 points total, so holding all eighteen skills at Novice costs 18 points, while one master costs a third of everything you have.

That last pair is the point of the design. Only three of your skills can rise above 75, and only one above 90, so nobody can be the best at everything. An ebony vein yields only to a Master Miner, so choose the trade you want to be known for.

The eighteen

Every skill on the Wheel, with what each of its five tiers unlocks. A tier starts at the level shown: Novice at 1, Apprentice at 25, Journeyman at 50, Expert at 75 and Master at 90. Eight skills open at a station; the other ten are taken up by doing the work.

Skills you learn by doing

A trade opens at its station: touch a forge and you are a smith. The other ten skills have no station to walk to, so they open through the work itself. Use one before you hold it and your effort is saved. Once it adds up to a level, the skill is offered to you:

“You have fought often enough this way to call it your own. Open your skills (K) to take up Unarmed.”

Taking it up in your skills menu (K) costs one point, and the effort you saved is credited to it, up to two levels at once, so it pays to accept soon. Nothing is spent until you accept, so a stray arrow never costs you a point. If all 300 of your points are in use, the point comes from a skill you have marked Waning (see below).

How points are earned

Only real work counts, and the server checks every piece of it: a craft at a real bench, a vein you actually worked, a fight you actually fought. Bigger work counts for more, such as a costlier craft, a costlier spell or a richer ore.

Each skill can take in only so much work an hour, so steady play beats a frantic rush. Doing the same thing to the same target again and again counts for less: the ninth time within an hour is worth half, and logging out does not reset that. A starving character learns at a quarter pace. The Leveling Guide has the numbers.

How points are lost

Nothing fades while you are away. A skill only falls when another rises past your 300 points and has to take them from somewhere, and you choose where from under the two moons, Masser and Secunda. Mark each skill you hold in your skills menu (K):

◒Waxing
Rises with use. Gives way only when nothing is waning.
●Held
Never falls. Your trade is safe while you swing a sword.
◓Waning
The first to give way when something else rises.

Nothing ever drops below level 25. If no skill can give way, the rising skill simply stops and you are told to mark a skill to fall. Taking up a new skill with every point in use works the same way, except that only a Waning skill pays for it: the point comes from your highest Waning skill, and with nothing Waning you must mark one first.

What happens to existing characters

Characters from before the Wheel keep their standing. Each skill converts the first time it is read and never lands below the tier it already held, and long service in a tier can carry it into the next. The old three skills start Waxing and everything else Held, so nothing starts slipping without your say, and you can hold more than three skills while you have points free.

Make your name on the Wheel

In most games you can become everything. Here you become someone. Every character has 300 points to spread across eighteen skills, from the mine and the forge to Blade, Blunt, the Closed Fist, Arcane Arts and the Priest’s prayers. Dabble in all of them, or pour yourself into one.

Learn by doing. Eight trades open the first time you set your hand to their station: an ore vein, a forge, an alchemy lab. The other ten, from Blade to Scholar, are learned by doing: fight with a sword and the effort banks as you go, and once there is a level’s worth you are offered the skill in your skills menu (K).

Mastery that means something. Only three of your skills can rise above 75, and only one can take the Seat above 90. Nobody can be the best at everything, so players who master different trades need each other.

Your progress is safe. Nothing fades while you are away. When a skill you hold rises past your 300, the Wheel takes the difference from another, and you choose which under Masser and Secunda: keep your trade Held, let an old habit Wane, and no skill is ever pulled below 25.

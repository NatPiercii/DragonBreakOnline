The Nine and the Sixteen

Who a character may worship, what each god gives, and the ten spell records still owed to the Creation Kit.

How religion works

A character holds one god at a time and may only pray at that god's shrines. Praying is a mini-game the server issues and the server judges: three verses run in turn and the worshipper holds the prayer key from the first word to the last. A completed prayer always credits the Priest skill. It only rarely earns the blessing.

Gods and faiths

Offerings

Say /offer <gold> at your god's shrine (anywhere, for the older faiths) to leave an offering of 5 to 1000 gold. Half is consumed as the offering itself, and half goes to the treasury of the town it is made in, for the temple's upkeep. For the next hour your god listens more closely: your next prayer's chance of a blessing grows with the offering, up to double at 100 gold, and that prayer spends it.

The picker

The menu opens on its own the moment a character is out of the race menu with no god — and to every existing character too, since none of them has one. It is a watcher rather than a creation hook, which is why it catches both. /deity reopens it at any time.

Columns of names beside a reading pane: Divines keyed aqua, Princes amber, and the older faiths in a column of their own from the next client update, the same split the prayer widget uses. Each name carries its marks — yours, no shrine, unlawful — and the pane gives the sphere, the boon, and whether there is anywhere you could actually pray. The first choice is free and needs no shrine; a later turn is gated by the seven days and nothing else, because the brief puts conversion on a menu key rather than a pilgrimage.

The widget sends one thing, deityChoose with a nonce and an id. The server judges it and re-sends the whole panel with a notice, so the menu never decides anything itself and a stale nonce changes nothing — the same contract as the prayer round.

Two rules worth knowing. Auri-El is Akatosh under the Aldmeri name, so a worshipper of either may kneel at either's shrine — without that, a Snow-Elf-faithed character could never pray at all, his one shrine being in the Forgotten Vale. And Talos and every Prince are unlawful: Bruma is Imperial, the Concordat outlawed Talos, and Beyond Skyrim has already renamed its Great Chapel of Talos to the Cathedral of St Martin. The server says so once and nothing follows from it — whether the guards care is still an open decision.

Sheogorath is the one worth noticing. The Madgod has no blessing of his own and should not have one, so he hands over somebody else's, rolled fresh every prayer. It is the only boon in the list that is more lore-accurate as code than as a record, and the only one that needed no Creation Kit work at all. The older faiths

Not every people kneels to the Divines or bargains with the Princes. Seven older faiths are open to anyone, and none of them needs a shrine: kneel wherever you are and say /pray. The same three verses, the same Priest credit, one rest of an hour between prayers. Because they can be prayed to anywhere, their blessings come half as often and last half as long as a shrine god's. Each boon is its people's own gift, and the Dragon Cult and the Worm Cult are outlawed in the Empire.

Which gods can actually be prayed to

Measured, not assumed — py ck-mcp\shrines.py counts every reference of every shrine base in the load order. Twenty-five of the twenty-six can be prayed to today — in practice, all of them. The nine Divines always could; the Princes arrived on 20 September, when a statue of each was placed at the Namira shrine site as one central place to test worship.

Beyond Skyrim reuses the vanilla Skyrim shrine bases through its own chapels — ShrineofAkatosh stands in CYRBrumaCathedralofStMartin. That is why the Divines were reachable all along.

Auri-El alone has no shrine of his own here, and it costs his worshippers nothing: his single shrine is in the Forgotten Vale, but he is Akatosh under another name, so any Akatosh chapel in Bruma will hear him. Every character on the server has somewhere to pray.

The Nine and the Sixteen

Who a character may worship, what each god gives, and the ten spell records still owed to the Creation Kit.

How religion works

A character holds one god at a time and may only pray at that god's shrines. Praying is a mini-game the server issues and the server judges: three verses run in turn and the worshipper holds the prayer key from the first word to the last. A completed prayer always credits the Priest skill. It only rarely earns the blessing.

Gods and faiths

Offerings

Say /offer <gold> at your god's shrine (anywhere, for the older faiths) to leave an offering of 5 to 1000 gold. Half is consumed as the offering itself, and half goes to the treasury of the town it is made in, for the temple's upkeep. For the next hour your god listens more closely: your next prayer's chance of a blessing grows with the offering, up to double at 100 gold, and that prayer spends it.

The picker

The menu opens on its own the moment a character is out of the race menu with no god — and to every existing character too, since none of them has one. It is a watcher rather than a creation hook, which is why it catches both. /deity reopens it at any time.

Columns of names beside a reading pane: Divines keyed aqua, Princes amber, and the older faiths in a column of their own from the next client update, the same split the prayer widget uses. Each name carries its marks — yours, no shrine, unlawful — and the pane gives the sphere, the boon, and whether there is anywhere you could actually pray. The first choice is free and needs no shrine; a later turn is gated by the seven days and nothing else, because the brief puts conversion on a menu key rather than a pilgrimage.

The widget sends one thing, deityChoose with a nonce and an id. The server judges it and re-sends the whole panel with a notice, so the menu never decides anything itself and a stale nonce changes nothing — the same contract as the prayer round.

Two rules worth knowing. Auri-El is Akatosh under the Aldmeri name, so a worshipper of either may kneel at either's shrine — without that, a Snow-Elf-faithed character could never pray at all, his one shrine being in the Forgotten Vale. And Talos and every Prince are unlawful: Bruma is Imperial, the Concordat outlawed Talos, and Beyond Skyrim has already renamed its Great Chapel of Talos to the Cathedral of St Martin. The server says so once and nothing follows from it — whether the guards care is still an open decision.

No boon may be a dead stat

Everything here is player-run: no merchants, no dialogue, no barter, and no speech skill among the eighteen. Persuasion, Speechcraft and Pickpocket therefore do nothing at all, which killed four boons — one of mine and three of Bethesda's.

The older faiths

Not every people kneels to the Divines or bargains with the Princes. Seven older faiths are open to anyone, and none of them needs a shrine: kneel wherever you are and say /pray. The same three verses, the same Priest credit, one rest of an hour between prayers. Because they can be prayed to anywhere, their blessings come half as often and last half as long as a shrine god's. Each boon is its people's own gift, and the Dragon Cult and the Worm Cult are outlawed in the Empire.

Which gods can actually be prayed to

Measured, not assumed — py ck-mcp\shrines.py counts every reference of every shrine base in the load order. Twenty-five of the twenty-six can be prayed to today — in practice, all of them. The nine Divines always could; the Princes arrived on 20 September, when a statue of each was placed at the Namira shrine site as one central place to test worship.

Beyond Skyrim reuses the vanilla Skyrim shrine bases through its own chapels — ShrineofAkatosh stands in CYRBrumaCathedralofStMartin. That is why the Divines were reachable all along.

Auri-El alone has no shrine of his own here, and it costs his worshippers nothing: his single shrine is in the Forgotten Vale, but he is Akatosh under another name, so any Akatosh chapel in Bruma will hear him. Every character on the server has somewhere to pray.

The test site, and the trap it walked into

Beyond Skyrim had already built Namira's shrine — CYRNamirasShrineExterior, grid (23, 47), ninety-nine references of snow, bone piles, cobwebs, red-eye lights and evil cairns. On 20 September a statue of every Prince went in around it, so worship could be tested in one place instead of across a province. Clavicus Vile is there too, using the vanilla quest shrine from Haemar's Shame.

Thirteen of the fifteen went in as STATICS, and a static is scenery. The engine fires no activation on one, so the server never hears about it — and in the Creation Kit a STAT and an ACTI look exactly the same once placed. Only ShrineOfMalacath and the vanilla DA09MeridiaStatue were activators and worked.

The fix kept every placement where it was: ten new DBO_ShrineOf<Prince> activators at 125BFC–125C05, each cloned from DBO_ShrineOfHircine and given the exact mesh of the statue already standing there, and then all thirteen references repointed at them. Nothing moved and nothing looks different; the statue simply is the shrine now. Every mesh comes from man_DaedricShrines.esp or Skyrim.esm, both already masters of the plugin, so no master was added.

The records, as written

Done. Eleven blessing spells at DragonBreak Online Edits.esp:1209BF–1209C9, written by Edit Scripts\DBO_Blessings.pas, plus Unarmed's five long-missing marker spells at 1209CA–1209CE in the same sitting. Not one needed a new magic effect — every effect below already existed. AltarMaraSpellWHAnvil uses AlchFortifySmithing, which is the proof that an alchemy-family effect works inside a shrine spell; that is what made the last few possible.

Each is the same shape: copy AltarNocturnalSpell, rename it DBO_BlessingOf<God>, swap the one effect, keep CureDiseaseEffect, duration 28800. Worth knowing for the next one: that template is a fire-and-forget self spell carrying an 8-hour effect, not a constant-effect ability — which is exactly why Meridia could not have a turn-undead. In that shape it would fire once, at the shrine, and never again.

Every effect id above is :Skyrim.esm, and the plugin gained no new master — still 52, so no self-index shift. skills.json points at the new records, both server\data\ and the dev Data are in sync, and the server has restarted. It reports blessings 22 resolved, 4 server-side, 0 broken, which is the real check: those ids resolve against the live load order, not a test mock. DBO_Skill_unarmed_T1..T5 went in beside them, so boot finally reads unresolved: none and 18 skills have marker spells. Ten shrine activators followed at 125BFC–125C05, taking the boot line to 44 shrine ids, 24 reachable.
For whoever writes the next one. Three things each produced a silent exit 0 with nothing done, and all three are now handled by tools\run-sseedit-script.ps1: -D: and -P: must be quoted into the command line or xEdit reads the data path as E:\DragonBreak; -script: wants a full path or it falls back to a file picker and waits for a human; and template is a reserved identifier in xEdit's script engine. A run straight after a plugin change also takes around twenty-five minutes while xEdit rebuilds its reference cache — check CPU before assuming it has hung.

Still open

Whether the law has teeth. lawful: false is written onto Talos and every Prince, and private.dboLawful already exists with the guards reading it. Wiring the two together would make Talos worship and Daedra worship the first real crimes in the game, in the county where the Thalmor keep a Justiciar. Good story, big change.

Hermaeus Mora is the best thing left to build, and it is not Creation Kit work. “What you read teaches you more” lands exactly on the Scholar skill and the reading mini-game; no other Prince's sphere maps onto an existing system that cleanly. It needs the read weight multiplied in masterySystem, so a server rebuild.

A real menu key. The picker itself is built, but /deity is what opens it. A hotkey means a client rebuild and a player re-download, so it should ride along with the next one rather than cost a relaunch of its own.

Nothing here has been knelt at. The whole path is covered by server\tests\prayer-harness.js and by the boot log, and by nothing else.

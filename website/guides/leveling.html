Time to Mastery

What it actually costs to raise a skill from nothing to 100: hours at the anvil, days on the calendar, and the four things that quietly cut your rate.

The short answer

Progress is measured in hours of validated work on that one skill, not hours logged in. Wandering, talking and travelling are worth nothing. These are the numbers for work you actually do:

What one hour of work actually holds

Each skill carries its own bucket that fills at 30 units an hour and holds 20 at most. Every act you perform spends from that bucket, and when it is empty the work still happens — the sword still swings, the ingot still comes out — it just stops counting until the bucket refills.

Two consequences worth knowing. First, the bucket is per skill, so alternating between the forge and the mine costs neither one anything — two skills can run at full rate in the same hour. Second, because it holds 20 units, a player who logs in after a break has about forty minutes of stored progress waiting; short sessions are not punished.

Bigger work is worth more than 0.5. A finished mining round is worth 1.0 for copper and up to 2.0 for ebony, and a skinned hide 1.0 to 2.0 by the value of the pelt. A finished chopping round, a finished reading, a finished prayer and a picked lock are worth 1.0 each. A craft is worth 0.5 to 3.0 by the value of what you make, a spell 0.5 to 1.5 by its magicka cost, a blow you take in armour 0.5 to 2.0 by how hard it hit, and an animal you kill 0.5 to 2.0 toward your Skinner by its level. A cooked dish is worth more the more goes into it, from 0.75 for a one-ingredient dish up to 3.0. A blow you land or a plant you pick stays at 0.5, and eating teaches nothing. Heavier work does not raise the 30 an hour; it fills the hour with fewer acts. So a Novice miner needs ten copper veins a level rather than twenty, and the better the seam the fewer it takes.

The four things that slow you down

1. Doing the same thing over and over

Repeating the same recipe, the same ore vein or the same target is worth less each time: the ninth in an hour is worth half, and it bottoms out at a third. Grinding one iron dagger over and over needs roughly 180 acts an hour to fill the same bucket that 60 varied acts fill. Move between veins, vary what you forge, hunt different beasts.

2. Starving

At hunger 90 your work counts for a quarter until you eat. Hunger climbs 12 an hour online, so a long unbroken session reaches starving in under eight hours. Carry food; it is the cheapest rate increase in the game.

3. Not having taken up the trade

Eight trades open at their own station: a forge, an alchemy table, a chopping block, an ore vein, a loom, a tanning rack, an arcane enchanter or a cooking pot. The first time you use one, that trade opens for one point from your pool. Until it is open, nothing you do for that trade counts.

The other ten skills (the six combat skills, Priest, Scholar, Lockpicking and Harvesting) keep a tally of your work instead. Once you have done a level's worth, your skills menu (K) offers the skill, and taking it up costs one point and gives it a head start from the work you banked. Arcane Arts and Priest also open when you study a Novice spell tome at a spell study point, and choosing a school of magic opens Arcane Arts (see Schools of Magic).

If your pool is already full, the point comes from a skill you marked Waning. With no Waning skill above 25, the new skill stays closed until you mark one.

4. The daily ceiling, once you are high

Below 75 the ceiling is 360 units, which needs twelve hours on one skill to reach — effectively no limit. From 75 it drops to 180 (six hours), and from 90 to 60 (two hours). That is the wall, and it is deliberate. A character also has a daily total across all skills together, 1,080 units (36 hours of work), which only matters if you work several skills all day.

One more, for groups: a party of more than six is a raid, and while it lasts every member's work counts for half.

Mages have one more meter to watch: casting a spell of your school of magic also raises that school, which takes in only so much casting a day. The Schools of Magic guide explains the schools.

90 to 100 takes fifty days, minimum. Three thousand units of work against a ceiling of sixty a day. Played perfectly, with no wasted act and no day missed, that is seven weeks. A masterwork smith is a person the server watched become one.

Breadth or depth

You hold 300 points across all eighteen skills, one point per level. Since the buckets are separate, the only real question is where you spend your hours. Two hours a day for a month buys either of these:

How you spend itAfter 30 days
All 2 h on one skillThat skill at 75 — Expert
30 min each on four skillsFour skills at 35 — all Apprentice, none notable

Only three skills may sit above 75, and only one of those may pass 90. In practice the pool binds first: a Seat at 100 with two Experts at 89 already spends 278 of your 300, leaving 22 points for everything else you touch. Choose the trade you want to be known for before you spend a hundred hours on it.

Points only ever leave a skill when a gain would push you past 300, and you choose which skills give way in your skills menu. Skills marked Waning give way first, highest first, and skills marked Held never give way. Once nothing Waning is left above 25, your lowest Waxing skill above 25 gives way instead. Nothing falls below 25, nothing decays while you are offline, and if nothing can give way the gain is refused with a notice rather than taken silently.

The playtesters’ boost

Everyone who helped test DragonBreak Online before the alpha gets double skill progress for 24 hours, starting at their first login after the alpha opens on 3 October at 05:00 UTC. The 24 hours run in real time, online or not, and cover every character on the account. Log in by 10 October at 05:00 UTC to claim it; /boost shows the time left. The usual limits still apply: the hourly bucket and the daily ceilings count your work as before, and each bit of it simply moves your skill twice as far.

Working it efficiently

  • Eat first. A quarter rate is worse than any other mistake on this list.
  • Bring variety, not volume. Sixty different acts beat two hundred identical ones.
  • Work two trades in the same session. Separate buckets, no penalty — smelt while the mine respawns.
  • An hour a day beats seven hours on Sunday once you pass 75, because the ceiling resets daily and unspent days are simply gone.
  • Stop at the ceiling. Past 90, two hours is the whole day's allowance; the third hour is for roleplay, not progress.
